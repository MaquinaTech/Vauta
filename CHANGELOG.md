# Changelog

All notable changes to Vauta are documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and Vauta follows [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- **Correct a transcript line.** Highlight a line in a meeting's **Transcript** and press Return (or click the pencil at its end, choose **Correct Line…** in its menu or **Edit › Correct Highlighted Passage**), fix the words and press Return; Esc cancels and ⌘Z takes the correction back. The line then says **Corrected**, shows what was transcribed on hover, and offers **Restore Original**. Search, Ask, the people and projects found in the meeting, the exports and the minutes written from then on read the correction; the line's translation is hidden while it is corrected, since it translates the words that were transcribed. Nothing the model wrote is rewritten: a decision, task or sentence of a note's expansion that relied on the words you changed (a name, a figure, a word added next to them) is marked **Unconfirmed**, "You corrected the transcript here after the minutes were written", until you check it, edit it or write the minutes again, and the mark goes when the words come back. A change of case, accents or punctuation, or of a hesitation such as "um", marks nothing. A question heard in the line follows the correction, and so do the follow-up meetings found in the meeting (a corrected "Tuesday" makes the suggestion a Thursday; what you dismissed or added stays), and a corrected line split between two speakers keeps what was transcribed for each part. When the words you replaced (a misheard name or term, such as Ana → Anna) are elsewhere in the meeting, the line offers **Correct Everywhere**: one click corrects them in the other lines (each keeps what was transcribed) and in the summary, topics, open questions, decisions and tasks, keeping who wrote each item; a mark the change answers goes. Nothing changes until you click it, and ⌘Z takes it all back. When the correction puts in a name or term Vauta doesn't know yet (one or two words, such as Kubernetes or API), the line also offers **Add “Kubernetes” to Glossary**: the term is pinned in the glossary in Context, so the custom vocabulary gives it to dictation and a later meeting that seems to mishear it gets a suggestion; ⌘Z takes it out again. The JSON export marks a corrected line with `"corrected": true`; what was transcribed is never exported, since a correction may be there to take a word out. It works with copy protection on (the field takes typing and pasting, not copying out), and not in the meeting being recorded.
- **Misheard names in English, French, Italian and Portuguese.** When the transcript writes a name or term of your Context as it sounds ("Cobaltics" for Kobaltix, "Tibo" for Thibaut, "Kiara" for Chiara, "Joakin" for Joaquim), Vauta now suggests the right one in these languages too, with the sound rules of each: only Spanish, Catalan and Galician had them, and elsewhere a suggestion needed a spelling one or two letters away. Names that sound different stay apart ("Hannah" and "Anna", "Jean" and "Jeanne", "René" and "Rennes"), and it still only suggests. On 1,314 common names and product names of the four languages, the 42 pairs it brings together by sound alone all sound alike, such as Thibault and Tibo or Xavier and Chavier.
- **Ask finds a name where the transcript misheard it.** When a question names a person or project of your Context, Ask also looks at the lines where the transcript wrote that name as it sounded ("Cobaltics" for Kobaltix), whichever way it searches. Once you accept Vauta's correction, they count as the name itself, in every meeting. While the correction is still open they count half, in that meeting only. A correction you turned down no longer counts.
- **Pause and resume a recording.** **Pause** under the panel's timer, or **Recording › Pause Recording**, stops capturing the microphone and the meeting's sound (macOS turns off the microphone indicator) while the meeting stays open; **Resume** goes on in the same transcript. The meeting's Transcript shows where it was paused (**Paused 12:03 – 12:10 (7 min)**), so the gap is never taken for a silence; the Markdown, text and PDF exports show it in the transcript too, and the JSON lists it under `pauses`. While paused there are no meters, no silence warnings and no automatic stop; marks and notes still work, and Stop saves the meeting as usual. The menu-bar icon shows a pause sign, and VoiceOver says when the recording is paused and resumed. If the microphone can't start again, the recording stays paused and says why.
- **Delete a transcript line for good.** **Delete Line…** in a line's menu (or ⌫ on the highlighted line, or **Edit › Delete**) asks first, then takes the line out of the record: its words go with what was transcribed when you had corrected it, its translation, the questions heard in it and the follow-ups found in it (the follow-up meetings are then looked for again in what is left); stored answers drop what they said from it, and the meeting's search vectors are made again from what is left. The indexes are merged, the freed space is overwritten and the store is written at once, as when a meeting is deleted, so nothing in Vauta keeps the words. Nothing the model wrote is rewritten: the decisions, tasks and note sentences written from the line are marked **Unconfirmed**, "You deleted the transcript line this was written from", for you to check or delete, and **Rewrite Minutes** writes the minutes without it. It can't be undone, and a backup made before keeps the line. Not in the meeting being recorded.
- **Delete a stretch of the transcript at once.** Highlight a line, then ⇧-click another (or press ⇧↑ or ⇧↓) to highlight every line between them, and **Delete 12 Lines…** in their menu (or ⌫) takes the whole stretch off the record after one confirmation, which gives the times it starts and ends. The lines go together, in one step and one write of the store, the same way **Delete Line…** takes one line, so a stretch is never left half deleted. A click, or ↑ and ↓ alone, ends the stretch. VoiceOver offers **Highlight Through This Line** on each line. Not in the meeting being recorded.
- **Every backup is checked, and Verify Backup… checks one later.** Once **Back Up…** has written the file, Vauta reads it back from the disk and opens it with the password exactly as a restore would, in memory, keeping nothing; the sheet then says **The backup opens with this password, and nothing in it is damaged.** A file that fails is deleted and the sheet says to choose another place. **Settings → Privacy & Security → Verify Backup…** runs the same check on any backup file without restoring it and shows what it holds: meetings, saved voices, document sources, and whether it carries your settings. It reads nothing of this Mac's data, so it needs no Touch ID and works while recording.
- **Your own voice, sent back by the call, is marked too.** When a participant on speakers sends your voice back (their call app doesn't cancel the echo), the transcript had your words again a moment later in **Others**, as if they had said them. Such a line is now marked and dimmed, **Your voice, sent back**, and left out of the minutes, answers, owners, search and exports, like the echo of your own speakers; **Not an Echo** takes the mark back. It is looked for also when you wear headphones. On simulated echoes over 24 public meetings it found 81 % of the words sent back and marked 0.05 % of the other participants' own lines. Meetings recorded before are not checked again.
- **The speakers' echo.** With speakers instead of headphones the microphone hears the other participants, and Vauta wrote their words again as yours, so the minutes gave you their words and their tasks. Every finished recording now marks those lines. They stay in the transcript, dimmed, with **It Was Me** to take a mark back (it can be undone), and they are left out of the minutes, notes, answers, owners, search, talk time and exports. A line the person says is theirs is never marked again. A recording in which the microphone never heard the others (headphones) marks nothing. Meetings recorded earlier, and a recording a crash left open, are checked once at the next launch, in the background. Their minutes are not rewritten: **Rewrite Minutes** writes them without the echo.
- **Pause with a shortcut, and a reminder.** ⌃⌥P pauses and resumes the recording from any app, and **Recording › Pause Recording** shows it. Like the shortcuts to mark a moment and take a note, it is taken only while recording, and it can be changed in **Settings › General**, which says when another app holds it. When a pause goes on for 15 minutes, a notification says the recording is still paused, with **Resume** and **Stop and Save**, so a meeting that went on is not missed for long; it comes once per pause and goes when the recording resumes or stops.
- **Update Now** in the panel's Live notes card writes the live notes at once from what was said since the last ones, instead of waiting up to 3 minutes. It is dimmed when nothing new was said; while the notes are written the card shows a spinner, and a second press never starts a second run (one kept while your own notes are expanded starts with the next line).
- While recording, the panel suggests headphones when the meeting plays through speakers: the Mac's own, a display, AirPlay, or any output the microphone is heard to pick up, such as Bluetooth or USB speakers. **Dismiss** hides it for the recording.
- **The exports say when the participants were informed.** When you marked that the participants were told the meeting was being recorded, the Markdown, plain-text and PDF minutes print **Participants informed** with the time under the date (JSON already had it). The export sheet offers **Say when the participants were informed** for such a meeting; it is on by default, and JSON writes null when it is off.
- **Minutes templates of your own.** **Add Template…** under **Your templates** in **Settings → Models** takes a name and, in your own words, what the minutes should cover and stress (for example, the feature requests and who asked for each). Your templates are offered with Vauta's: as the default template, in a meeting's **Rewrite Minutes** menu, and for a recurring meeting in **Context**, whose new meetings then use it (a recurring meeting can take one of Vauta's templates too). Wherever they are offered, and on the minutes they wrote, they are marked **Custom · not evaluated**: Vauta measured its own templates on meetings, not these. The minutes keep their sections and every decision and task is still checked against the transcript; your instructions reach the model as your text, which can't change its rules. Apple Intelligence and other models with a small context read the first 600 characters or so of a template of up to 4,000 (about 200 in Chinese, Japanese or Korean). Deleting a template sends the meetings and recurring meetings that used it back to the default; a recurring meeting made from a repeating calendar event keeps its template when its last meeting is deleted. When a title points to one of Vauta's templates ("1:1 with Ana", "Daily stand-up", "Entrevista", "面接"), the meeting's **Rewrite Minutes** menu offers it first and a recurring meeting's form suggests it, in the nine languages; nothing is written with it until you choose it. Templates are kept in the encrypted store and in backups, and wait for Touch ID like the Context.
- **Translated transcripts.** **Translate** in a meeting's **Transcript** reads a finished meeting in another language: a meeting held in Japanese, say, read in English. The lines keep their words, with the translation in gray under each one, and choosing another language shows that one. Apple's translation does it on this Mac (a language that isn't on the Mac yet is downloaded first, after macOS asks); with **Improve finished sentences with your model server** on in **Settings → Translation**, your model server translates each line with the three before it, after any other request, and Apple's translation takes the lines it can't. Progress shows under the transcript's controls, with **Stop**, and nothing is translated while a meeting is being recorded. A line you correct is translated again; **Translate Again** redoes every line. Exports that include the translation take the language the transcript shows and say which one it is and that it is a machine translation (Markdown, plain text and PDF; the JSON adds `translationLanguage`). Each translation is stored with the words it was made from, so one never shows under a line that changed since.
- Importing a recording older than the transcripts are kept (or dated in the future) says before importing that the meeting will be dated today, and why; it used to change the date without a word.
- **Drop a recording on the Meetings window to import it**, as with **File › Import Recording…**: one audio or video file, which then opens the import sheet. The window's edge shows where it can be dropped; several files, a folder or a document are refused.
- **Ask reads the time a question names.** "What did we decide last week?", "¿Qué se dijo ayer?" or "先週の会議で何を決めましたか" now look only in the meetings held then, and a control under the question says so (**Only meetings from Oct 19 – 25, 2026**); clicking it searches all meetings again. A time that may be what the question is about rather than when its meetings were ("what is due this week", "last month's figures", "in October") is only offered there, off. Deadlines ("by Friday") and times still to come are ignored. When those meetings have nothing that matches, or there are none, Ask searches all meetings and says so. It reads English, Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Japanese, Korean and Chinese, on this Mac and without a model. On the 420 archived evaluation questions, which name no time of meeting, it applied to none and offered two.
- **Ask follows up.** After a question, the next one can go on from it: "And who owns it?", "¿Y para cuándo?" or "那谁负责？" are read together with the question before (**Following up on “What did we decide about the launch?”**), so search finds what the two are about, and the time or meetings the earlier question named carry over when the new one names none. The control is on when the question reads as going on (a word that continues, or a short question that points back, in the nine languages) and off but there to turn on otherwise; clicking it asks again. The answer is still written and checked for that one request, and nothing is stored: the questions before are forgotten when Ask is cleared or Vauta locks.
- **Ask says what it looked at.** Under an answer about all meetings, one line tells how many meetings were searched and their dates, how many of them had passages sent to the model (and how many documents), how many were searched by keywords because they aren't indexed for search by meaning yet, and how many older meetings no longer have a transcript, so they weren't searched. It is counted from what the request held, also when nothing was found.
- **A speaker who says who they are is asked about.** "Hi, I'm Ana", "Soy Ana, de Kobaltix", "私はアナです" or "我叫王伟" give that speaker the question "Is this Ana?" when Ana is a person in Context or invited to the meeting (by her full name, an alias or her first name when nobody else has it), with what they said under it; it is a name only when you say yes. It reads the start of a clause in the nine languages (an opinion such as "I'm sure" or someone else's name, "I'm Ana's manager", gives nothing; "this is Ana" counts only after a greeting, as on a call). It never touches a speaker you named or cleared, a speaker a voice already suggests, a person you said no to for the meeting or yourself, and a speaker who gives two names, or a name two speakers give, is left alone. It runs when the speakers of a recording are written and with **Find Names in This Meeting**.
- **A speaker someone speaks to by name is asked about.** "What do you think, Ana?" answered by a speaker, "Ana, can you share your screen?" followed by one, or "Thanks, Ana" after one spoke give that speaker the question "Is this Ana?", with what was said under it (**Speaker 1 said to them: “What do you think, Ana?”**, or **You said to them: …** when you asked). It reads the nine languages without a model, and only a name said to someone in a question, a handover ("over to you, Ana", "adelante, Ana") or thanks: a remark ("That's your fault, Ana"), a name talked about ("I'll ask Ana"), a possessive or a list of people gives nothing. A question points to the first speaker who talks after the name is said, thanks to the one who spoke before, both within ten seconds, and never to a line whose speaker is a guess; a person counts only when at least twice as many of those turns point to one speaker as to any other. The same rules as for introductions apply, and what a speaker says of themselves comes first. On the public AMI meetings it suggested 11 people in 22 meetings and none in the scripted ones; read against the lines around each, all 11 fit what was said.
- **Ask finds "the last meeting".** "What did we decide in the last meeting?", "la reunión anterior", "the last three calls", "the first meeting with Ana" or "前回の会議" now look only in those meetings, and a control under the question names them (**Only “Weekly sync” (Oct 28, 2026)**); clicking it searches all meetings again. One meeting is read whole, as when you ask about it. "With Ana", "about Kobaltix" or "where we discussed Kobaltix" count only the meetings that name a person or project from Context; without one Vauta knows, the question searches all meetings as before. "The first meeting" counts only with one of them (the first of a project, not the first ever recorded). "Since the last meeting" is a point in time and is ignored, and so are questions that compare meetings ("between the third and the last meeting"). With a time ("the last meeting last week") the place is within it. On the 420 archived evaluation questions it found one place, a council's "acta de la sesión anterior", and none in the 27 questions across meetings.
- **Ask checks the figures of its answer's text.** Each quoted point already had to have its figures in the moment it quotes; now the answer's own sentence must too, against everything the model was given (the passages with their dates and times, the documents, the question). A figure that is in none of them marks the answer **Partly verified**, the note under it names the figure, and a live answer with one is not shown. A true figure from another passage still counts. On 1,749 archived verified answers it flagged two: a figure the transcript never says, and a year written as "2015,17".
- **Ask reads the minutes of older meetings.** After 90 days a meeting keeps its minutes but not its transcript (when the knowledge is kept longer than that). Ask now sends the decisions, tasks and summary of those minutes that match the question, after the passages of the transcripts, and checks each quoted point against the item it cites, word for word. Such a point is marked **From the minutes of an older meeting**, with its own symbol instead of the green seal: the minutes summarize what was said, they are not its words. Its citation reads "Weekly sync · Minutes" and opens the minutes, and the line under the answer says that only their minutes were searched. A time whose meetings only have their minutes is searched there before all meetings are.
- The times in a meeting's **Minutes** tab (decisions, marks and the lines that cite a moment) preview the line said then, as the citations of Ask do: rest the pointer on the time, press Space while it has the keyboard focus, or use VoiceOver's **Preview Passage**. A link with nothing to preview no longer offers that action.
- **A preview of the cited passage.** In an answer of Ask, resting the pointer on a citation for a moment, pressing Space while it has the keyboard focus, or VoiceOver's **Preview Passage** action shows the passage it cites: who said it and the words, the question's words marked, with **Show in Transcript** (or **Open Minutes** for an older meeting's minutes). Copy protection applies to it too.
- **Find in Meeting.** ⌘F in a meeting opens a bar over its transcript that finds a text as typed, anywhere in a word, ignoring case, accents and width, so it also finds what the meeting search would not (a function word, part of a code). It says how many places there are (**3 of 12**), marks them all with the one chosen in a solid marker, highlights that passage and scrolls to it; Return, ⌘G and ⇧⌘G move to the next and previous place, starting from the passage you had highlighted, and a menu limits the places to one speaker. VoiceOver hears the count on each move. **Search Meetings** moves to ⌥⌘F; ⌘F still searches the meetings when no meeting is shown.
- **Search Settings.** The Settings sidebar has a search field, as in System Settings: type part of a setting's name ("vocabulary", "shortcut", "back up") and it lists the panes and settings that have it, each with its pane, in the language of the app; choosing one opens that pane.
- **This Week.** Tasks has a second view, **This Week**: the week's meetings, the decisions taken in them, the tasks added and done in it, the tasks that are overdue now and the open questions, each opening where it came from. It is read from what is stored, with no model, so it says exactly what the minutes and tasks say (not the items the minutes set aside), in the week of your calendar (its first day and time zone). The arrows go back a week at a time, and **Export…** saves it as Markdown (not with copy protection on).
- **Answers to questions heard read the time and the meeting they name.** A question heard while recording, such as "What did we decide last week?" or "¿Qué dijo Ana en la última reunión?", now looks for its answer first in the past meetings it names, as Ask does, and in all of them when nothing there matches. Only a time that Ask would use by itself counts: there is no control to turn one on during a meeting. The meeting being recorded is never "the last meeting".
- **A model server's certificate is renewed in time.** **Settings → Status** turns yellow 30 days before the HTTPS certificate of a server on your network ends (the server's own, an intermediate or your private CA, whichever ends first), says the date and that `make-server-cert.sh` renews one it made and keeps the pin. When it has ended, the connection error says so and how to renew it, instead of a general TLS failure. `Vauta doctor` prints until when the certificate is valid.
- **A meeting's menu in the list.** Right-clicking (or Control-clicking) a meeting in the Meetings list offers **Export…**, **Copy Minutes**, **Copy Minutes as Plain Text** and **Delete Meeting…** for that meeting, the same commands as the File and Edit menus. The meeting is opened first, as a click would, so the confirmation counts its own transcript, decisions and tasks; a meeting being recorded can't be deleted from there. VoiceOver reaches the menu with VO-Shift-M.
- **Hide Vauta's windows while you present.** **Recording › Hide Vauta Windows** (or **Hide Windows** at the bottom of the panel while recording) takes Meetings, Settings and Vauta's other windows, the Notepad and the captions off the screen at once while the recording goes on, and gives the keyboard back to the app you were in. Nothing of Vauta comes on screen by itself meanwhile, a window you open on purpose shows as usual, and the menu-bar icon shows a crossed-out eye. **Show Vauta Windows**, or **Show Windows** in the panel, brings back what was on screen, stacked as it was. This doesn't rely on macOS keeping windows out of screen sharing, which it no longer guarantees for every sharing app.
- **Choose several meetings in the list** with ⌘-click or ⇧-click (Meetings mode). The detail says how many are chosen, and **Delete 2 Meetings…** (there, in the list's menu, in **Edit** or with ⌫) deletes them together after one confirmation, which says that reminders and events Vauta created from them stay; it is off while one of them is being recorded. Choosing one meeting, or opening one from anywhere else, shows it as before. In Ask the list still picks one meeting.
- **The meeting companion.** While recording, a small bar stays beside your meeting app with the recording's time and its controls: **Mark a Moment**, the notepad, **Pause** and **Stop**, one click each, without taking the keyboard or the front from the meeting app (the menu-bar panel closes as soon as you click elsewhere; the bar stays). It opens with each recording, can be dragged anywhere and stays where you put it; its close button puts it away for the rest of the recording, and **Recording › Show Meeting Companion** brings it back. **Settings → General** turns it off. **Hide Vauta Windows** takes it off the screen with the rest.
- **The meeting companion's details.** **Show Details** on the companion opens, under its bar, the answers to questions heard, the live notes and the newest passages of the transcript, the panel's own cards kept beside the call when the panel closes. Like the bar, they never take the keyboard or the front from the meeting app, and what arrives never moves the focus. **Hide Details** folds them away, and the choice is kept for the next recordings (off by default: folded, the companion shows no meeting text).

Developer tools:

- `Vauta diarize --none-bias <n>` (and `--none-bias` in `eval/scripts/run_diarization.py` and `run_diar_sets.py`) lowers the segmentation's "nobody speaks" class by that much before each frame is decided, to measure calling more frames speech. Swept from -0.5 to 2 on the tuning set and the held-out English files: missed speech turns into as much false alarm or more, and the best value is within noise, so the app keeps the network's own decision (docs/BENCHMARKS.md, "Tried and not changed").
- `llm-eval --tasks ask-cross` asks each question in the meetings it names by its time or place, as Ask does, with "now" the day after the series' last meeting; its traces say which (`scope`) and whether nothing there matched (`widened`). The live answers' replay writes `pastScope`.
- `llm-eval --tasks echo` also sweeps the far side's echo judged in Others and writes `echo/far_sweep.tsv`.
- Five tests that failed now and then on the CI runners no longer depend on how fast the runner is. One found the race of the document folders fixed above (its wait now also waits for a scan in flight). Two had races with the wall clock: a hand-off dated before a slow line, and a warm-up interval that passed between two lines. On the runners' virtual machines (`TestMachine.isVirtual`), two bounds measured on a Mac are widened, never dropped: the logits of the tiny model, where the paravirtual GPU sometimes rounds differently, and the speaker finalizer's debug time budget.
- `Vauta llm-eval --tasks ask-retrieval --after-meeting --items <minutes folder> --item-key-weight <w>` stores the decisions and tasks of minutes `minutes-eval` kept and uses them as keys of Ask's retrieval: a decision or task that matches the question brings in the moment it cites, never its own text. On the harness dev questions, with the server's minutes, weight 2 found the gold of 1 more decision question of 29 and 2 more task questions of 13 but lost 3 fact questions of 62, so the app keeps the keys off (`MeetingStore.defaultItemKeyWeight`).
- `Vauta llm-eval --tasks token-count --sets <transcripts> --out <dir>` compares the token estimate every budget uses with the count of Apple's on-device model (`tokenCount(for:)`, macOS 26.4 or later) on public transcripts, without generating anything. On the 24 harness dev transcripts the estimate is 12% over Apple's count in English and 24% in Spanish, and up to 2% under it in a few English windows; it is not changed ([LANGUAGE_MODEL.md](docs/LANGUAGE_MODEL.md#apples-model)).
- `Vauta llm-eval --tasks echo` measures the speakers' echo detection on public transcripts, with no audio and no model. Each participant is replayed as the Me channel with the others' speech heard again 0.03, 0.15 or 0.4 s later and 10–30 % of its words wrong, and once more with the far side sending every line back. It writes the score of every setting to `echo/sweep.tsv`. On the 24 DEV meetings the release settings find 90.7 % of the echo's words and mark 0.30 % of the participants' own lines.
- `Vauta echo-coupling <me audio> <others audio>` measures, over two files, whether a recording's microphone heard the others, as the app does while recording. `eval/scripts/run_echo_coupling.py` runs it on the 10 AMI meetings: no meeting heard over headphones, and none taken for headphones when the microphone heard the others, even as faint cross-talk ([BENCHMARKS.md](docs/BENCHMARKS.md#whether-the-microphone-heard-the-others)).
- `Vauta llm-eval` writes the replies of a minutes run that failed to `minutes/<meeting>.<provider>.failed.json`, so the cause can be read.
- `Vauta transcribe-file --show-text --out <words.jsonl>` also writes every recognised word with its time and the recognizer's confidence, which the app itself does not ask for. `eval/scripts/run_wer.py --confidence` uses it on public audio to measure what asking costs (WER and time against the plain pass) and how well the confidence predicts a right word (reliability bins, ECE, Brier, AUROC), before anything in the app relies on it. On the 10 AMI meetings asking changes no word and takes no longer, and the confidence tells right words from wrong ones with an AUROC of 0.79 on the headsets and 0.77 on the remote mix ([BENCHMARKS.md](docs/BENCHMARKS.md#word-confidence)).
- `Vauta llm-eval` can write the minutes of public transcripts and answer their questions on a model server (`--providers server --server <config.json> [--ask-key]`), through the app's own client and address rules, and runs several minutes variants of one backend interleaved in one run (`--variants old,new`).
- Ask on a model server can reason briefly before an answer after the meeting (`low` effort, with its own room and no schema over it). It stays off in this release until it is measured: `llm-eval --tasks live-answer --providers server --after-meeting --ask-reasoning on,off` asks each question both ways in one run.
- The statistics the evaluation gates use (Wilson intervals, exact sign and McNemar tests, meeting-cluster bootstrap, kappa, Jeffreys gates, paired TOST, Holm and BH) are in `tools/llm-eval/stats.py`, standard library only, with tests that CI runs.

### Changed

- **The integrated model reads a prompt faster and with less energy.** Gemma's last 18 layers reuse the keys and values of earlier ones, and only the last position's result is ever read, so they now run on that one position instead of on every token of the prompt; while a chunk's result is not needed at all, they don't run. On the small test model the same prompt is read in 35% less time with the same result; the real model, where 18 of 42 layers share, has not been measured again yet. This speeds up the minutes, Ask and live answers, and the reading of the meeting while it records.
- While recording on battery, the integrated model reads the meeting ahead every 3 minutes with new text, as in Low Power Mode, instead of every 20 seconds: each reading costs GPU energy that the minutes spend anyway when the recording ends. On the charger nothing changes.
- A task owner given by a speaker whose voice probably mixes two people is marked **owner not confirmed**. Speaker separation can put two similar voices, or one person's line, in another person's speaker, and the minutes then gave that person the other's task with nothing to say so. Vauta now keeps how tight each speaker's voice is (one number, not the voice), and the owners that a loose speaker's lines give stay, marked. A line you moved to a speaker yourself is not marked. Meetings whose speakers were separated earlier are not marked.
- `install.sh` and `update.sh` check what the build needs before starting it (Apple silicon, macOS 26, the Command Line Tools with the macOS 27 SDK and Swift 6.4, about 8 GB free) and stop with what to do, instead of failing minutes into the build; they build with 3 jobs at a time on a Mac with 24 GB or less (2 with 8 GB), so compiling MLX does not run out of memory. `VAUTA_SKIP_PREFLIGHT=1` skips the checks (`scripts/preflight.sh`).
- **By meaning** in the meetings list also works with a filter (a person, a series or the last days). It looks only in the filtered meetings, and it ranks by meaning once those meetings are indexed, even while the rest of the library still is. As with the keyword results under a filter, it shows no documents. Before, a filter turned it off.
- The integrated model keeps its tokenizer and the index its structured output uses when it is unloaded after 5 idle minutes, so the first question after a quiet spell no longer reads, checks and indexes them again (3.7 s in a debug build: 1.5 s for the tokenizer and 2.2 s for the index); memory pressure and deleting or replacing the model still let them go. The index is now built while the weights load instead of before the first token.
- With a model server whose structured output is "JSON schema" or "JSON object" (Settings → Models → Advanced), the minutes' block extracts, merges and closing request and the live notes ask for their shape in `response_format`, as Ask already did. The requests with reasoning on send none. A server that answers 400, 415 or 422 to it is asked once more without it.
- Restoring a backup builds its search indexes again from the text before anything reads them, instead of checking the ones in the file, so a crafted index is never parsed; a backup whose indexes have settings Vauta never sets is refused, and the restored indexes always delete for good. Restoring takes about as long as before. `Vauta doctor` and **Copy Diagnostics** show the version of the system's SQLite.
- **The meetings list is in sections by day**: **Today**, **Yesterday**, **Earlier This Week**, **Earlier This Month**, then one per month (with its year when it isn't this one), counted in your calendar, with your first day of the week and your time zone. A row under Today or Yesterday shows only the time, and one earlier this week the weekday and time, since the header already says the day. The sections move on at midnight and when Vauta comes back after a sleep.
- The downloads from Hugging Face (the search model and the integrated model) send `User-Agent: Vauta` instead of the system's default, which also named Vauta's build and the versions of macOS and of its network stack. `build-app.sh` (and so `install.sh` and `update.sh`) checks the pinned dependency before building and builds only from `Package.resolved`, stopping if it doesn't match instead of resolving again.

### Fixed

- **Meetings** relock on time when the Mac's date or time is changed: the unlock now counts on the system's continuous clock instead of the date, so setting the clock back no longer kept Meetings unlocked longer (the time the Mac sleeps still counts).
- A backup file declaring an image larger than any Mac can hold made Vauta quit while restoring it; it is now refused as damaged. Fuzz tests now feed mutated input to the parsers of a model server's stream, the store's journal and the store image, and check that each ends in a value or an error within its limits.
- A file added to a document folder while Vauta was scanning it is no longer missed until the next change in that folder. A change that arrives during a scan lists the folder again when the scan ends, and **Reindex** asked during a scan still reads the skipped files again.
- A recording no longer fails to start for a speech model that Settings shows as installed, with no way to download it. After an update re-signs an ad-hoc build, macOS still lists the model as installed, but Vauta can no longer use it. Settings, the first-run setup, `Vauta languages` and `Vauta doctor` now count a model as installed only when Vauta can use it, so they offer the download the recording needs.
- A recording that stays on "Starting…" can be given up with the record button. After a minute it says what it waits for, with the Settings pane that fixes it, and quitting no longer waits for it. It waited without end on a step macOS held: after an update re-signs an ad-hoc build, macOS asks again for system audio, in a dialog that can sit behind other windows.
- The first seconds of a recording are no longer lost while the speech analyzers start. The microphone and the system audio start first, and their audio waits for the analyzers.
- A stall or a gap in the system audio no longer "corrects" its sample rate when the rate was right. The rate is measured on the capture times, and a correction needs two windows that agree.
- Custom vocabulary no longer asks the recognizer for the misspellings Vauta learned from accepted corrections. Vauta still recognizes them as the name.
- Undo and **Remove from Reminders…** no longer ask whether you changed a reminder or event there when you didn't. On a busy Mac the item could be saved more than ten seconds after Vauta recorded it was adding it, and it looked edited. The ten seconds now run from the save.
- Ask no longer marks a claim as verified only because its quote is in the transcript. Every figure of the claim must be in the quoted moment too, written with digits or in words ("ninety one percent" for 91 %), as the minutes already required. A claim of 50,000 that quotes a moment saying 5,000 now shows as not verified: "the quote is there, but a figure in this sentence isn’t". A citation code in the claim ("segment S73") or a speaker label is not a figure. On 3,256 answers of earlier evaluation runs on public meetings, 37 claims lose the mark and no claim whose figure is in its moment does. Examples: "decree 111/21" for a moment that says 110/21, or a quota number the moment never says.
- Searching the meetings list in Japanese, Chinese or Thai finds a word in the middle of a sentence, in transcripts, decisions, tasks and notes. Those languages are written without spaces, and the search found only the start of a sentence. Ask already found them.
- On a model server, once the reasoning of the minutes has run past the length limit, the one repair of an answer that can't be read no longer reasons again. Two DEV meetings spent another 10,000 tokens, some ten minutes each, there.
- On a model server whose reasoning keeps running past the length limit, the minutes no longer spend it every time. After two minutes in a row whose reasoning ran out, the next five are written without it, then Vauta tries again. With qwen3.5-9b on MLXServer, 49 of the 51 reasoning requests of the 24 DEV meetings ran out, some ten minutes each and 70 % of the run's time, and those minutes came from the request without reasoning anyway.
- A task the model writes with the speaker's label ("Speaker 5 to build the dictionary") is no longer "to be confirmed" because the label's number is not in the quoted words, and the new extraction no longer drops it as reported speech. On a model server (qwen3.5-9b, the 21 DEV meetings, replayed) the reference's firm tasks and decisions found among the confirmed items rose from 29 % to 44 %, while the confirmed items that match the reference went from 57 % to 54 % (within the noise). Apple's model rarely writes items that way: 2 of 24 meetings changed.
- On a model server without structured output, the minutes had almost no open questions: the model wrote them as objects, the way it writes decisions, and Vauta skipped them. They are read now, and the usual checks judge them. On the 24 DEV meetings (qwen3.5-9b, replayed) the open points of the reference found in the minutes rose from 4 of 123 to 44 of 123.
- A model server with a key no longer asks for access to the key at every request. After an update re-signs an ad-hoc build, macOS asks once more for each Keychain item, and choosing **Allow** instead of **Always Allow** asked again for every request to the server, often several dialogs at once. Vauta now reads the key once per launch and keeps it in memory.
- `scripts/update.sh` stopped with `unbound variable` before verifying the release, in shells that read the `…` after a variable name as part of the name. If it stopped for you, run `git checkout main && git pull` once and run it again.
- `scripts/update.sh` ended with `dir: unbound variable` and exit status 1 after a successful install, and left a temporary folder behind.
- Checking the model server (Settings → Status, Test Connection, or installing the search or integrated model) no longer cancels minutes or an answer that are on their way.
- A request to the model server that raced with a new server configuration could close the app. It now ends as cancelled.
- Ask within one meeting, and a search in a meeting's materials or in one source, no longer miss that meeting's or those files' passages when the rest of the library has more than 400 closer passages on the same topic.
- When a follow-up email draft is handed to the mail app through a `mailto:` link, the system log no longer receives the link (with the addresses, the subject and the body, marked private). It records only the number of recipients and the link's length.
- The notepad's note in Settings → General and the documentation no longer say that Vauta's windows are always hidden from screen sharing: Vauta asks macOS to keep them out, which is not guaranteed. The documentation also corrects other claims the code no longer supports, among them the integrated model's download in SECURITY.md.
- `Vauta minutes-file` (an evaluation command) measures the minutes the app actually sends to Apple's model, and its result file names the parts that ran.
- Minutes no longer fail when Apple's model declines part of a meeting, or a part does not fit the model's window. A meeting declined as a whole is written by blocks; a declined block is left out and counted, a declined merge keeps its group, a final request that does not fit goes by the digest, and a digest declined twice keeps the checked decisions, tasks, topics and questions without a title or summary. Only a meeting whose every block is declined fails, and it says the model declined.
- On a model server, minutes whose reasoning runs past the length limit are asked once more without reasoning, at the same limit, instead of with twice the limit. With Qwen3.5-9B on an MLX server the closing request's reasoning ran to the 10,000-token limit and then to about 20,000 (some 30 minutes in all), and the minutes failed.
- `Vauta server-check --ask-key` stops and says so when nothing was typed at the key prompt, instead of sending the requests without a key and reporting that the server rejected it.
- With the search model, **By meaning** in the meetings list and a meeting's related documents no longer show a passage only because it holds a code or a rare name from the search, or because it has no vector yet. They now keep a passage only when its meaning is close enough, as the brief already did. Exact matches stay in the keyword results.

## [0.9.3] - 2026-10-28

Fixes from a review of the code after 0.9.2, and a safe way to update.

### Added

- `scripts/update.sh` updates Vauta to the newest release: it verifies that the release tag is signed by the maintainer's key (against the `.github/allowed_signers` already on your Mac and the fingerprint in SECURITY.md), refuses to go back to an older version, shows the release notes, runs the tests and installs with the same bundle ID and signing method. From a version before 0.9.3, run `git pull` once first.

### Fixed

- Pressing Stop now stops the microphone and the system audio at once. Before, they kept running while a note being typed was saved, so a few words said right after the stop sound could reach the meeting.
- A backup could pass the schema check with SQL hidden behind comment markers inside a quoted name (`"--"`), such as an extra trigger that would then run in your store. The check now reads quoted names whole.
- A model server on another computer given by name (for example `gpu.local`) needs a private CA or a certificate pin, as documented, also when the pasted configuration or the `server-check` file has no server type. Only a configuration saved by 0.1.x keeps working without them, with a warning; editing it asks for the CA or pin.
- Backup passwords of 12 or more characters but fewer than 6 different ones (such as `111111111111`) are refused, as the hint already said.
- A second clone of Vauta, or a new folder, on a Mac where `setup-signing.sh` had already finished was told to delete the signing keychain, which changes Vauta's signature and makes macOS ask for every permission again. `setup-signing.sh` and `install.sh` now find the existing identity and use it.
- `install.sh` and the README no longer say to deny every later Keychain prompt: macOS asks again after each update of an ad-hoc build and after a new signing identity, and denying it then leaves Vauta without its store.

Recording:

- Me and Others now share one clock. Each channel's audio is placed by the time it was captured, so a gap (a Bluetooth headset dropping out, the microphone being reconfigured, the system audio being reconnected) stays a gap instead of shifting that channel for the rest of the meeting, and the transcript stays in order. Marks, notes and live notes use the same clock, so a slow start (for example the first microphone prompt) no longer moves them later than the transcript.
- If on-device transcription fails in the middle of a meeting, that channel is restarted at the same point (up to three times) instead of staying silent for the rest of the meeting.
- Saving a meeting can no longer stay in "Saving" forever: finishing the transcription, the speaker separation and the speaker names each have a time limit.
- A lost microphone is tried again every few seconds while recording, so one that comes back late is used again.
- Stopping while a recording was still starting no longer leaves live answers running, and what was transcribed during a cancelled start is not kept (notes typed meanwhile are).
- Live speaker labels are recomputed less often as a meeting grows (meetings under about 7 minutes are unchanged), so they keep up in meetings of several hours.

Model servers:

- For a server given by name, nothing with content or the key is sent until Vauta knows the address it connects to is on your network: a first request without either checks it. A later new connection is checked when it completes, as before, and the private CA or pin still protects it.
- "On the same network" addresses are accepted only for IPv6; an IPv4 address must be private.
- Checking a server's certificate never fetches anything from a certificate authority, with or without a private CA. A server with a public certificate must send its full chain (`tailscale cert`, Caddy and `fullchain.pem` do).
- A model loaded with less than 8K of context is no longer shown as 8K: Settings says it is too short, and `server-check` warns when a model is loaded with less context than configured.
- Error messages from a server never show a key or an `Authorization` header.
- Finding servers on this Mac and reading a streamed answer stay fast and bounded with a misbehaving server.

Backups and your data:

- A restore interrupted by a crash or a power cut no longer leaves the store unreadable: the new key is kept next to the old one until the restored data is on disk, and the next launch finishes the restore. The key item is written anew with Vauta's own access list.
- Saving a backup over an existing file never truncates it if the write fails, and cancelling no longer deletes the new file after the old one was replaced.

Meetings, actions and documents:

- Due days on a day that has no midnight in its time zone (for example 6 September 2026 in Chile) are no longer dropped.
- Follow-up meetings read from the transcript more than a year away are no longer suggested, and a past date no longer hides a later one in the same sentence.
- Opening a linked material or a brief's file that is only in iCloud or Dropbox no longer downloads it: Vauta says it is online only, as it already did elsewhere.
- Numbers the model writes in the minutes no longer take the Mac's number format: 2026 stays 2026, never "2.026".
- VoiceOver reads "Document" for a citation without a file name instead of nothing.

Install and release:

- The version comes from `Resources/Info.plist`, so a clone without the newest tag builds the right version; the release workflow refuses a tag that doesn't match it.
- A throwaway build can use its own bundle ID (`VAUTA_BUNDLE_ID=local.vauta.dev`), so it never opens your installed Vauta's data.
- `install.sh` opens Vauta when the same build is already installed, and a protected Trash no longer stops it after installing; `setup-signing.sh` locks the signing keychain however it ends.

## [0.9.2] - 2026-10-28

### Fixed

## [0.9.0] - 2026-10-27

The first public release (public beta). Compared with 0.1.0, Vauta is open source, works with any meeting app and any language Apple supports, and can write minutes with Apple's on-device model or an optional integrated model. Evaluation figures and their limits are in [docs/BENCHMARKS.md](docs/BENCHMARKS.md) and [docs/CLAIMS.md](docs/CLAIMS.md).

### Added

**Recording and capture**

- Capture the other participants from any meeting app or browser (Safari and WebKit, Chromium and Gecko browsers, Zoom, Teams, Meet, Slack, FaceTime, Webex, Discord and more). The audio source is **Auto** (the app using the microphone), chosen apps, or all system audio; the tap follows the app's helper processes while you record. See [docs/COMPATIBILITY.md](docs/COMPATIBILITY.md).
- A capture alert when a meeting app holds the microphone but nothing reaches Others for a minute, with what to check.
- Calendar events are recognised by their meeting links, and the call suggestion names the app and the meeting service.
- Transcription in every language Apple's speech recognizer supports on your macOS, with a language per meeting and speech models installed from the app.
- A consent helper: a dismissible notice to paste into the meeting chat, in the meeting's language, and a record of when participants were informed.

**Speakers**

- Speaker separation on this Mac with two small bundled neural models (pyannote community-1 segmentation and WeSpeaker ResNet34-LM, CC BY 4.0): *Speaker 1*, *Speaker 2*… while recording and in imports, refined when the recording stops. On by default; it never delays transcription. The bundled models are checked against pinned SHA-256 hashes, nothing is downloaded, no audio is stored, no voice is kept between meetings and no network path is added. Diarization error rate fell from 10.8% to 9.5% on 19 held-out English recordings and from 9.4% to 7.2% on 12 synthetic Spanish conversations ([BENCHMARKS.md](docs/BENCHMARKS.md#diarization-error-rate)).
- Name speakers in the transcript; names flow into the minutes, task owners, search, Ask, a person's card and exports (SRT, WebVTT, Markdown, text, PDF, JSON).
- Opt-in voice recognition across meetings (**Settings → Speakers**, off by default): a saved voice only suggests who is speaking ("Is this Ana?") and never names a speaker by itself. Voice data stays in the encrypted store and in backups you make (restoring one brings its voices back, including voices you deleted after the backup), and never appears in exports, logs, prompts or the network. Voice summaries kept with a meeting last 14 days at most.
- `Vauta diarize` and `Vauta speaker-models` commands.

**Minutes, Ask and notes**

- **Apple Intelligence** (Apple's on-device model) can write minutes and answer questions with no setup, labelled "Quick start · Basic quality".
- An optional **integrated language model**: Google's Gemma 4 E4B-it, run inside the app on MLX, for Macs with 16 GB or more. It is never the default and is downloaded only when you click Download (a 15 GB transfer from Hugging Face, quantised on the Mac to about 4.2 GB and verified against pinned hashes). While you record it reads the transcript incrementally, so minutes and answers start sooner. See [docs/LANGUAGE_MODEL.md](docs/LANGUAGE_MODEL.md).
- Guided model server setup (*Set Up a Model Server*) for LM Studio or Ollama, with a model suggested for the Mac's memory, and automatic detection of servers on this Mac (LM Studio, Ollama, llama.cpp, MLX, vLLM, Jan, LocalAI) with per-server profiles.
- Minutes templates (general, one-on-one, stand-up, client call, interview, class), minutes in the meeting's language or a chosen one, progress and cancel, retry, and minutes for meetings interrupted by a crash.
- Every decision and task is checked against the passage it cites (figures, dates, names); task owners come from the transcript. Items that do not hold up are marked **Unconfirmed**. On Apple's model the decisions and tasks are ranked and the rest are shown apart, collapsed, under **Other possible items**.
- **Notepad**: shorthand bullets typed during or after a meeting become complete notes, each sentence citing the transcript; your own words are never rewritten.
- **Live answers** (Settings → Recording): questions asked aloud are noticed on this Mac and answered in the panel with verified, cited claims. Off by default on every model, and not offered with Apple's on-device model. Apple's model and the integrated model failed the evaluation gates (2% and 21% of shown answers judged correct, against 85%), and a model server has not been measured.
- An optional **search model** (EmbeddingGemma 2 by Google, 574 MB) for search by meaning across languages, downloaded only on request and verified against pinned hashes. Ask, the sidebar search and related documents rank by meaning when it is installed. Cross-language questions are translated on this Mac for keyword search.
- **Documents** as knowledge sources (PDF, Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Markdown, text, RTF, HTML, CSV and more) for Ask and the brief. Files kept online only are skipped and counted; Vauta never downloads them.

**Before and after a meeting**

- A **pre-meeting brief** with Calendar on: who is coming, what the last meeting left open, related meetings, documents, notes and reminders, built on this Mac. A notification (10 minutes before by default) says only that a brief is ready. A model-written agenda is off: no backend passed its usefulness gate. See [docs/BRIEF.md](docs/BRIEF.md).
- **Follow Up…** (⇧⌘U) turns a meeting's open tasks into reminders and agreed follow-up meetings into Calendar events, one confirmed item at a time, with Undo and protection against adding a task twice. Vauta suggests at most 2 follow-up meetings found in the transcript. See [docs/ACTIONS.md](docs/ACTIONS.md).
- Built but off in this release, because they did not pass their judged evaluation: the follow-up email draft and links between a task or decision and the same one in an earlier meeting.

**Meetings window and app**

- Meetings, Ask, Tasks and People modes, search, export (Markdown, plain text, PDF, SRT, WebVTT, JSON), import of audio and video recordings, and an editor for people, projects, clients, topics, glossary terms and recurring meetings.
- **Live Translation**: a captions panel that translates the other participants into your language with Apple's Translation framework, optionally improved by your model server; translations are stored with the transcript.
- **Back Up… / Restore…**: all meetings, context and settings in one file encrypted with a password you choose. Model server keys are never included.
- A first-run setup, a redesigned menu-bar panel and Settings, notifications without meeting text, configurable shortcuts, a relock time for the Meetings window, and accessibility labels and keyboard navigation.
- Interface translations: Spanish, French, German, Italian, Brazilian Portuguese, Japanese, Korean and Simplified Chinese.
- Command line: `doctor`, `languages`, `help`, `--version`, and `--locale` for the speech commands.

**Privacy, auditing and open source**

- **Network Activity** (Settings → Privacy & Security): a live list of every connection Vauta makes, with its purpose and destination. All network access goes through one audited code path, enforced by a test.
- Apache License 2.0, `NOTICE`, SPDX headers, unit tests, continuous integration, CodeQL and OpenSSF Scorecard workflows, and issue and pull request templates.
- An evaluation harness under `eval/` (pinned public corpora, diarization error rate and word error rate, held-out sets) and `docs/CRYPTO_REVIEW.md`, an internal review of the cryptography (not an audit).

### Changed

- Vauta is distributed as **source code only**: you clone the repository and build it on your own Mac. There are no signed or notarized downloads.
- One third-party package: a pinned fork of MLX (MIT), which brings in Apple's swift-numerics; otherwise Apple frameworks and the system SQLite. `scripts/check-dependencies.sh` enforces it.
- The minimum system is **macOS 26** (0.1.0 needed macOS 27).
- New name and tagline: "Your meetings, locked in your Mac."
- Data from an earlier version (0.1.0, bundle ID `local.acta.app`) carries over when you build with that bundle ID: the store files are renamed in place on first open (verified with the key first; nothing is deleted), and the server key moves to a new Keychain item. Backups are now `.vautabackup`; older backup files still restore. Builds from source use the bundle ID `local.vauta.app` unless `VAUTA_BUNDLE_ID` is set.
- The interface, command-line output, prompts and source code are in English, and the interface is localized.
- New installs capture **Auto** by default; an upgrade from 0.1.0 keeps capturing Safari only until you change it.
- Copy protection is a setting in Privacy & Security: off for new installs, on for installs upgraded from 0.1.0.
- Privacy wording was checked in every language, and `scripts/check-strings.sh` fails if a translation promises more than the English text.
- Store updates are more durable (full syncs, directory syncs after renames, Keychain items updated in place), and errors explain what happened and what to do.

### Fixed

- Minutes written with Apple's on-device model no longer fail with "cut off at the length limit" on meetings of 30 to 60 minutes.
- A brief task is "Overdue" only when its due day is before today.
- Text that was cut, colour contrast and VoiceOver names found in a review of every screen in nine languages, light and dark.
- Leaving a call is detected promptly; a headset that is also the clock device is read correctly; transcription warnings clear when the channel recovers.
- Rewriting minutes keeps your edits; merging or deleting a person updates every reference; search keeps acronyms and short names.
- Answers cut off at the token limit, or replies that are not JSON, are retried once; a locked Keychain or a model still downloading no longer uses up retries.

### Security

- Model downloads use a dedicated network purpose and an exact destination allowlist (`huggingface.co` and its named download host, no wildcards); redirects are never followed automatically, and nothing is requested at launch.
- Downloaded models are data, not code: bounded, fuzzed parsers, no pickle and no remote code, and no entitlement was added for MLX.
- Changing the model server's mode, address, CA or pins removes the saved key instead of sending it to the new destination.
- Stricter local-network rules for model servers: proper IPv6 parsing, IPv4 addresses with leading zeros rejected, and host names require your own CA or a pinned key. The address is checked again after connecting, which stops the requests that follow but not the first one; that is why host names need your own CA or a pin.
- Every certificate in a CA bundle is trusted as an anchor, and a bundle with a malformed certificate is rejected.
- `doctor` and `store-stats` never create a store or a key.
- Erase Meetings and Context removes the key and the data from this Mac, but older backups of the login Keychain may still hold the old key.
- Model downloads send no token or identifier, and the signed download address and its query string are never logged.

### Removed

- Apple's on-device embeddings (`NLContextualEmbedding`), the old "Search by meaning on this Mac · Experimental" option: in every benchmark it found passages worse than keyword search. A saved choice moves to keyword search and its stored vectors are deleted.
- The `store-selftest` command; its checks run in the test suite.
